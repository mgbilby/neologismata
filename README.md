# Neologismata gitbook

## Origination and Version History

Neologismata was authored by Mark G. Bilby [orcid:0000-0003-0100-6634](https://orcid.org/0000-0003-0100-6634) and first publicly archived as Version 1.0 on 2026-04-18 on Zenodo at [base doi:10.5281/zenodo.19646263](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19646263). Version 1.1 was archived 2026-04-19 on Zenodo. 

Richard Carrier wrote a critical review covering about 15-20 pages of the book in a blog post made on 2026-05-01. The review was entitled [“Problems with the Bilby Thesis”](https://web.archive.org/web/20260515155327/https://www.richardcarrier.info/archives/41567). Neologismata version 1.2 incorporated Carrier's feedback and was archived 2026-05-15 on Zenodo.

## Description

A NEOLOGISM — a NEW WORD — creates a rippling in Time. It splashes forward upon its surface of porous fabric; echoes in waves, spoken and written, until it enters our ears and minds. Neologisms hide in plain sight, embedded among more commonplace words. Words that at first sounded new and creative eventually fade into the background, with origin points and meanings increasingly difficult to pinpoint over time.

Massive Corpus Linguistics projects, however, are changing this.  Using a database-driven approach — ingesting, correlating, and querying these corpora —, Dr. Bilby has built a data-magnet to detect neologistic needles hiding in these high and heavy lexical haystacks. In this book, Bilby pinpoints over 40 non-Christian neologisms as tiny treasures, micro-features, trace elements that together give us our clearest picture yet of the dates when documents in the NTwere composed, edited, and/or reached stable form.

If and when Bilby's findings are confirmed by other scholars, it could radically change New Testament studies. The canonical New Testament documents were not finalized in the 1st or even early 2nd century. These forty neologisms show they were composed and/or still being significantly edited between 138 and 249 CE.
