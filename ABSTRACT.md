Neologisms (newly minted and/or popularized words) --- could they be the missing key to determine the actual dates of the canonical New Testament writings? 

In this work, Mark G. Bilby combines database-driven Greek lexicography  with interdisciplinary subject-matter expertise in early Christianity, late antique Judaism, and Greco-Roman Classics to forge a new methodology that can unlock the origin points of Greek words. 

The data sources include the Thesaurus Linguae Graecae, databases of Greek papyri and inscriptions, and custom databases making use of major, open access Greek linguistic corpora, namely, the Opera Graeca Adnotata (Leipzig) and GLAUx (Leuven). 

Using this methodological key, Bilby unlocks what were heretofore obscure and unknown editors/sources behind over a hundred apparently new words used in the canonical New Testament. 

For some forty of these words, the sources happen to be the most famous non-Christian Greek authors writing between 138 CE and 249 CE. For the rest, the sources are largely well-known early-Catholic authors. Their combined and cumulative influence radically shifts the Overton window of the dating of composition and final editing of the canonical New Testament to the late-2nd to early-3rd centuries CE.
